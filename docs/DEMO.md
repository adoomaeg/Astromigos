# Demonstrating Astromigos

## Before presenting

Start the app, leave the simulated-data badge visible, and explain that all live health readings and individual medical-history examples are fictional. The public portraits identify demonstration profiles, not real patients.

The simulator starts with synthetic history so baseline comparisons are available without waiting several days. Those mission-day values are not an account of the real Artemis II flight.

## A short presentation

1. **Introduce the idea.** Explain the goal: connect measurements to understandable problems, suggested next steps, and a review that closes the loop.
2. **Meet the crew.** Select different portraits and show that each person has an individual status and history workspace.
3. **Explore the body.** Select a monitored system, inspect the measurements, and contrast it with a reference-only structure.
4. **Show a controlled problem.** Choose Acute cardio event at 1 min/s. Allow simulated minutes to pass until the critical message appears.
5. **Review the response.** Read the suggested steps. Open Tools, then Problem & follow-up tracker, or choose Track follow-up on a recommendation.
6. **Log an action.** Explain that the intervention only has a hypothetical effect inside the simulator. The case remains open while signals or rules remain abnormal.
7. **Follow up.** Add an observation note. After relevant signals clear, show the written-review requirement before resolving the case.
8. **Show the individual history.** Open Medical history and locate the demonstration note and current-run events.
9. **Explain the boundary.** Finish by distinguishing a working interface and simulation from clinical validation or an operational mission system.

## Scenario guide

| Scenario | Subject | Suggested speed | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominal day | All crew | 1 min/s or 1 h/s | Activity context, personal ranges, and stable status. |
| Acute cardio event | Reid Wiseman demonstration profile | 1 min/s | Combined changes, critical escalation, and human-review guidance. |
| Sensor failure | Victor Glover demonstration profile | 1 min/s | Degraded or missing readings, unknown status, and sensor-check steps. |
| Cabin CO2 rise | Shared cabin | 1 h/s | Environment readings and co-occurring crew signals. |
| Progressive fatigue | Christina Koch demonstration profile | 1 h/s or 6 h/s | Multi-day sleep and recovery changes. |
| Exercise performance decline | Jeremy Hansen demonstration profile | 6 h/s | Performance across successive exercise sessions. |

Use slower speeds for an acute-event explanation. At 6 h/s, a minute-scale change can pass too quickly to inspect. Pause the simulation when discussing a specific state.

## Earth-link demonstration

Compare ISS or Moon support with a Mars communication profile using the Earth-link tool. Show the modeled message transit time and the onboard-first guidance when ground review is delayed. Messages and replies are generated by the simulator; no real flight surgeon is contacted.

## Reset behavior

Restarting or changing scenarios creates a new simulation run. Current-run alerts, recommendations, and tracker context restart. User-entered medical-history records may remain in browser storage. Different browsers or devices do not share those records.
