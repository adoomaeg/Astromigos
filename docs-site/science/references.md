# Scientific References

This is the consolidated reference list for the Science section of the Astronaut Health Intelligence Platform documentation. Each entry was opened and checked on **2026-11-14** (access date for every link below): title, authors or issuing office, date and venue were compared with the landing page (official NASA or CSA page, NASA Technical Reports Server record, or PubMed record for journal articles). Footnotes in the Science pages point to these entries by their ID.

::: info How to read this list
"Supports" says which statements in the documentation rely on the source. Dates are the publication date or the "last updated" date shown by the page on the access date. Official web pages can change; journal articles are identified by DOI and PubMed ID (PMID).
:::

## Spaceflight hazards and risk frameworks

- <a id="r1"></a>**[R1]** NASA Human Research Program. *5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight*. NASA; page last updated 2026-06-12. <https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/>. Supports: the five hazards framework, hazard descriptions, interaction between hazards, environmental factors of closed habitats.
- <a id="r2"></a>**[R2]** NASA Human Research Program. *Hazard: Distance From Earth*. NASA; page last updated 2024-08-01. <https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-distance-from-earth/>. Supports: Mars average distance of 140 million miles, communication delay of up to 20 minutes one way, need for crew self-sufficiency.
- <a id="r3"></a>**[R3]** NASA Human Research Program. *Hazard: Gravity Fields*. NASA; page last updated 2024-08-01. <https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-gravity-fields/>. Supports: bone mineral density loss of 1% to 1.5% per month, headward fluid shift, pressure on the eyes, kidney stone risk from dehydration, lightheadedness and fainting on return.
- <a id="r4"></a>**[R4]** NASA. *Risk of Adverse Cognitive or Behavioral Changes and Psychiatric Disorders Leading to In-mission Health and Performance and Long-term Health Effects (Behavioral Health Risk)*. NASA; published 2025-03-11, last updated 2025-09-17. <https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/risk-of-adverse-cognitive-or-behavioral-conditions-and-psychiatric-disorders>. Supports: effects of isolation and confinement on cognitive and behavioral functioning, status of countermeasures.
- <a id="r5"></a>**[R5]** NASA Human Research Roadmap. *Risk of Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS)*. NASA; last published 2025-05-13. <https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/risk.aspx?i=105>. Supports: definition and hallmark signs of SANS, 70% of crewmembers with one or more signs, ocular surveillance methods (OCT, ultrasound, imaging, visual acuity, MRI), active risk status.
- <a id="r6"></a>**[R6]** Schauer K. *Space Communications: 7 Things You Need to Know*. NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; 2020-10-06, last updated 2026-02-09. <https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/goddard/space-communications-7-things-you-need-to-know/>. Supports: Mars one way delay of about 4 minutes at closest approach and around 24 minutes at greatest distance.

## Radiation and cabin environment

- <a id="r7"></a>**[R7]** NASA Johnson Space Center, Space Radiation Analysis Group. *What is space radiation?* Last updated 2025-09-24. <https://srag.jsc.nasa.gov/spaceradiation/what/what.cfm>. Supports: galactic cosmic rays, solar particle events, trapped radiation belts, South Atlantic Anomaly (about 200 km, six or seven passes a day in low Earth orbit).
- <a id="r8"></a>**[R8]** NASA Office of the Chief Health and Medical Officer. *NASA-STD-3001 Technical Brief: Design for Ionizing Radiation Protection*. OCHMO-TB-020, Rev G; 2025-06-06. <https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-020-radiation-protection.pdf>. Supports: career effective dose limit below 600 mSv, solar particle event limit below 250 mSv, effective dose method, personal and area monitoring requirements, acute and long term radiation risks.
- <a id="r9"></a>**[R9]** NASA. *Why Space Radiation Matters*. NASA; 2017-04-13, last updated 2025-08-22. <https://www.nasa.gov/missions/analog-field-testing/why-space-radiation-matters/>. Supports: types of space radiation, cancer, central nervous system and degenerative risks, radiation sickness beyond low Earth orbit.
- <a id="r10"></a>**[R10]** NASA Office of the Chief Health and Medical Officer. *NASA-STD-3001 Medical Technical Brief: Carbon Dioxide (CO2)*. OCHMO-TB-004, Rev D; 2026-03-10. <https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf>. Supports: CO₂ removal on the ISS (CDRA), hypercapnia symptoms, headache data from Expeditions 2 to 31, 3 mmHg one hour average limit, ISS levels since 2016, cognition studies.
- <a id="r11"></a>**[R11]** NASA Open Science Data Repository. *Environmental Data App (EDA)*. <https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/eda/>. Supports: ISS cabin CO₂, temperature, humidity and radiation dose data (GCR, SAA, total, accumulated) for payload mission periods, downloadable tables.
- <a id="r12"></a>**[R12]** NASA Open Science Data Repository. *RadLab: Overview*. <https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/overview/>. Supports: description of RadLab as a single access point for radiation telemetry from several agencies, with export and an API. Availability was intermittent on the access date.
- <a id="r13"></a>**[R13]** NASA Science. *About OSDR*. Last updated 2026-10-23. <https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/data/osdr/>. Supports: scope of the NASA Open Science Data Repository (omics, physiological, behavioral, biomedical and environmental telemetry data) that hosts EDA and RadLab.

## Physiology in spaceflight (peer reviewed)

- <a id="r14"></a>**[R14]** Hughson RL, Helm A, Durante M. Heart in space: effect of the extraterrestrial environment on the cardiovascular system. *Nature Reviews Cardiology*. 2018;15(3):167-180. doi:[10.1038/nrcardio.2017.157](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrcardio.2017.157). PMID [29053152](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29053152/). Supports: gravity dependence of the cardiovascular system, postflight problems, possible late cardiovascular risk from radiation, exercise as a countermeasure.
- <a id="r15"></a>**[R15]** Barger LK, Flynn-Evans EE, Kubey A, Walsh L, Ronda JM, Wang W, Wright KP Jr, Czeisler CA. Prevalence of sleep deficiency and use of hypnotic drugs in astronauts before, during, and after spaceflight: an observational study. *The Lancet Neurology*. 2014;13(9):904-912. doi:[10.1016/S1474-4422(14)70122-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(14)70122-X). PMID [25127232](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25127232/). Supports: short sleep measured by wrist actigraphy in flight (about 6.09 h per night on ISS missions) and in preflight training, comparison with each crew member's own ground intervals.
- <a id="r16"></a>**[R16]** Lang T, LeBlanc A, Evans H, Lu Y, Genant H, Yu A. Cortical and trabecular bone mineral loss from the spine and hip in long-duration spaceflight. *Journal of Bone and Mineral Research*. 2004;19(6):1006-1012. doi:[10.1359/JBMR.040307](https://doi.org/10.1359/JBMR.040307). PMID [15125798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15125798/). Supports: areal bone mineral density loss of 0.9% per month (spine) and 1.4% to 1.5% per month (hip) in 14 ISS crewmembers; pre and post flight densitometry.
- <a id="r17"></a>**[R17]** Vico L, Hargens A. Skeletal changes during and after spaceflight. *Nature Reviews Rheumatology*. 2018;14(4):229-245. doi:[10.1038/nrrheum.2018.37](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrrheum.2018.37). PMID [29559713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29559713/). Supports: links between skeleton, fluid shifts, vascular system and metabolism; parallels with immobilization and ageing.
- <a id="r18"></a>**[R18]** Trappe S, Costill D, Gallagher P, Creer A, Peters JR, Evans H, Riley DA, Fitts RH. Exercise in space: human skeletal muscle after 6 months aboard the International Space Station. *Journal of Applied Physiology*. 2009;106(4):1159-1168. doi:[10.1152/japplphysiol.91578.2008](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.91578.2008). PMID [19150852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19150852/). Supports: calf muscle volume decrease of 13% on average in 9 crewmembers, exercise volumes on ISS, variation of exercise programs between crewmembers.
- <a id="r19"></a>**[R19]** Smith SM, Heer MA, Shackelford LC, Sibonga JD, Ploutz-Snyder L, Zwart SR. Benefits for bone from resistance exercise and nutrition in long-duration spaceflight: evidence from biochemistry and densitometry. *Journal of Bone and Mineral Research*. 2012;27(9):1896-1906. doi:[10.1002/jbmr.1647](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbmr.1647). PMID [22549960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22549960/). Supports: ARED with adequate energy intake and vitamin D maintaining bone in most regions during 4 to 6 month missions.
- <a id="r20"></a>**[R20]** Sibonga J, Matsumoto T, Jones J, Shapiro J, Lang T, Shackelford L, Smith SM, Young M, Keyak J, Kohri K, Ohshima H, Spector E, LeBlanc A. Resistive exercise in astronauts on prolonged spaceflights provides partial protection against spaceflight-induced bone loss. *Bone*. 2019;128:112037. doi:[10.1016/j.bone.2019.07.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bone.2019.07.013). PMID [31400472](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31400472/). Supports: ARED alone partially attenuates bone loss but not trabecular loss at the hip.
- <a id="r21"></a>**[R21]** Basner M, Savitt A, Moore TM, Port AM, McGuire S, Ecker AJ, Nasrini J, Mollicone DJ, Mott CM, McCann T, Dinges DF, Gur RC. Development and validation of the Cognition test battery for spaceflight. *Aerospace Medicine and Human Performance*. 2015;86(11):942-952. doi:[10.3357/AMHP.4343.2015](https://doi.org/10.3357/AMHP.4343.2015). PMID [26564759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26564759/). Supports: the Cognition battery (10 tests, cognitive domains), practice effects and the need for normative data.
- <a id="r22"></a>**[R22]** Lee AG, Mader TH, Gibson CR, Tarver W, Rabiei P, Riascos RF, Galdamez LA, Brunstetter T. Spaceflight associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS) and the neuro-ophthalmologic effects of microgravity: a review and an update. *npj Microgravity*. 2020;6:7. doi:[10.1038/s41526-020-0097-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41526-020-0097-9). PMID [32047839](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32047839/). Supports: clinical manifestations of SANS.
- <a id="r23"></a>**[R23]** Brunstetter TJ, Mason SS, Taiym WF, Gibson CR, Van Baalen M, Tarver WJ. *Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS): 2023 Clinical Update*. Presentation (abstract), 2023 NASA Human Research Program Investigators' Workshop, Galveston, TX. NTRS document 20220014606. <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220014606>. Supports: about 66% of long duration astronauts with the earliest indication of SANS.
- <a id="r24"></a>**[R24]** NASA. *NASA Fluid Shifts Study Advances Journey to Mars*. NASA; 2015-07-20. <https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/nasa-fluid-shifts-study-advances-journey-to-mars/>. Supports: fluid shift hypothesis for vision changes, non invasive measurement methods (CCFP, DPOAE, OCT, ultrasound).
- <a id="r25"></a>**[R25]** Garrett-Bakelman FE, Darshi M, Green SJ, Gur RC, Lin L, Macias BR, et al. (82 authors in total), Turek FW. The NASA Twins Study: a multidimensional analysis of a year-long human spaceflight. *Science*. 2019;364(6436):eaau8650. doi:[10.1126/science.aau8650](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau8650). PMID [30975860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30975860/). Supports: longitudinal before, during and after design with a matched ground control; carotid artery and ocular changes.

## Health monitoring on missions

- <a id="r26"></a>**[R26]** NASA. *Standard Measures* (Spaceflight Standard Measures). NASA; page last updated 2025-06-04. <https://www.nasa.gov/reference/standard-measures/>. Supports: list of standardized measures collected before, during and after missions.
- <a id="r27"></a>**[R27]** NASA. *Artemis II Standard Measures*. NASA; page last updated 2026-03-17. <https://www.nasa.gov/reference/artemis-ii-standard-measures/>. Supports: Artemis II measures (samples, MRI, vestibular tests, muscle performance, microbiome, physical function tests); more than 30 ISS astronauts in Standard Measures since 2018.
- <a id="r28"></a>**[R28]** Canadian Space Agency. *Bio-Monitor: Keeping an eye on astronauts' vital signs*. Date modified 2026-02-26. <https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/bio-monitor.asp>. Supports: Bio-Monitor components and measured parameters, launch to the ISS in December 2018, development by Carré Technologies.
- <a id="r29"></a>**[R29]** Canadian Space Agency. *Wireless body sensors for medical care in space*. CSA blog; 2015-12-23. <https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/blog/2015/12/23/wireless-body-sensors-for-medical-care-in-space.asp>. Supports: Astroskin developed by Hexoskin (Carré Technologies) under CSA contracts.
- <a id="r30"></a>**[R30]** Toscano W, Cowings P, Sullivan P, Martin A, Roy J, Krihak M, Winther S, Hanson A, Shaw T. *Wearable Biosensor Monitor to Support Autonomous Crew Health and Readiness to Perform*. Presentation, 2017 NASA Human Research Program Investigators' Workshop, Galveston, TX; 2017-01-23. NTRS document 20190001996. <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190001996>. Supports: Astroskin testing in HERA (16 crewmembers, 24 hour monitoring), lessons on data loss and Bluetooth, no comparison with laboratory standard devices.
- <a id="r31"></a>**[R31]** Gaskill ML. *Wearable Tech for Space Station Research*. NASA, International Space Station Research Communications Team; 2024-11-13, last updated 2025-01-06. <https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/wearable-tech-for-space-station-research/>. Supports: Actiwatch Spectrum, T-Mini headband, sleep monitoring vest, personal CO₂ monitor, EVARM and Active Dosimeter.
- <a id="r32"></a>**[R32]** NASA Johnson Space Center. *Exercise Countermeasures*. NASA; page last updated 2026-01-22. <https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-exercise-countermeasures/>. Supports: ISS exercise devices (CEVIS, ARED, T2, COLBERT) and health monitoring items (blood pressure, ECG, heart rate).
