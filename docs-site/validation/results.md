# Results & Evidence

This page is the canonical record of what has been **measured** on the Astronaut Health Intelligence Platform (AHI). Every number comes from a generated file, a test run or an observation stated as such. How these results fit into a validation plan: [Validation strategy](./strategy).

::: warning What these numbers mean
All engine results are measured on **simulated data**, against the **same simulator that generated the data**. They show internal consistency of the prototype rules, not real world sensitivity, specificity or clinical performance. Nothing here is validated on real crews.
:::

## Engine evaluation

Source: `frontend/web/src/engine/evaluation.json`, generated by `npm run evaluate` (`scripts/evaluate.ts`) on 2026-11-14 with engine `ahi-engine 2.0.0`. The same run regenerates `docs/model-card.md`.

### Nominal false alerts

5 seeds (20261114 to 20261118) x 72 sim hours x 4 crew = **60 crew days**, analytics every 15 sim minutes. In the nominal scenario nothing is wrong, so every alert counts as a false alert.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Crew alerts at HIGH or above per crew day | **0** |
| Crew alerts at MODERATE or above per crew day | 0 |
| Cabin alerts | 0 |
| Highest attention level reached | MODERATE (briefly, without an alert) |
| Share of time at LOW / MODERATE / HIGH / CRITICAL | 99.97% / 0.03% / 0% / 0% |

### Scenario detection

Minutes from the scenario start (08:00 UTC sim time), on the evaluation grid (1 min for acute cardio and sensor failure, 5 min for CO2, 15 min for fatigue and performance decline). Level is the target astronaut's attention level (cabin scenario: worst of the cabin and crew levels).

| Scenario | Target | First alert (rule) | HIGH | CRITICAL | Peak (score) | Simulated run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ACUTE_CARDIO | AST-01 (ORION) | 7 min (R-HR-01) | 7 min | 7 min | CRITICAL (100) | 3 h |
| SENSOR_FAILURE | AST-03 (VEGA) | 14 min (R-DQ-02) | not reached | not reached | MODERATE (20) | 3 h |
| ENVIRONMENTAL_CO2 | cabin | 90 min (R-ENV-CO2-01) | 140 min (2.3 h) | not reached | HIGH (77) | 12 h |
| PROGRESSIVE_FATIGUE | AST-02 (LYRA) | 1905 min (31.8 h, R-FAT-01) | 2010 min (33.5 h) | not reached | HIGH (79) | 96 h |
| PERFORMANCE_DECLINE | AST-04 (ATLAS) | 1650 min (27.5 h, R-EX-01) | 4530 min (75.5 h) | not reached | HIGH (45) | 120 h |

**Reading the results.**

- The acute event is caught within minutes and is the only scenario that reaches CRITICAL, as designed.
- Slow scenarios are capped at HIGH by design: they call for review, not an emergency response.
- The sensor failure is a data quality case: it reaches MODERATE ("signal lost, status unknown") and never looks normal.
- The fatigue pattern needs more than a day of evidence before the combined rule fires, which is the intended trade-off against false alerts.

### An issue the evaluation found

The evaluation found and fixed a real problem: nominal false alerts on blood pressure during exercise warm-up. The fix is the upward only acute rule in the exercise context ([Anomaly detection](../intelligence/anomaly-detection#robust-z-score)).
